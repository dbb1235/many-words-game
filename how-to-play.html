Gerbil — how to play

Three racks. One clock. As many words as you can find.

Gerbil hands you three scrambles of ten letters each. Build the best word you can out of every rack before time runs out — solo against the clock, or head-to-head with up to five other players racing the exact same tiles.

The Basics

Building a word

Every rack gives you 10 tiles: 8 real letters plus 2 blank wildcards. Drag tiles down from the rack into the guess row underneath to spell something.

Q10
U6
I1
Z7

QUIZ — the U landed on a blue (triple-letter) slot, so its value jumped from 2 to 6. Word total: 24 pts.

Only the unbroken chain counts

Tiles are read starting from the very first slot in the guess row. The moment there's a gap, everything past it is just parked there — it isn't part of your word yet. Fill the gap and it joins in.

Reading the verdict

Below the guess row, your current word and its live score are shown in three states:

FIS still typing FISH 7 pts FSIH not a word

Undo and clear

  • Drag a placed tile back up to the rack, or double-click it to send it back instantly.
  • ✕ Erase clears the whole guess row in one tap — useful when a word's a dead end.
Tiles

Wildcards

?0

Unassigned — sitting in your rack.

E0

Double-click a placed wildcard to choose its letter.

A wildcard always scores 0 points, no matter which letter it stands in for. Use it to unlock a word you couldn't otherwise spell — not for points.

Tiles

Bonus slots

Some slots in the guess row are colored instead of plain white. The color is the label — no icon needed:

R2

Green = double letter. Whatever tile lands here has its point value doubled.

R3

Blue = triple letter. Whatever tile lands here has its point value tripled.

The bonus belongs to the slot, not the tile — move that tile off and the multiplier stays behind for the next thing you put there. So it's often worth planning which letter goes on the colored square, not just which word you spell first.

Scoring

Letter values

Every letter carries a point value — common letters are worth less, rare ones are worth more, same idea as a classic tile game.

Two rules that gate what counts

  • Your word has to be 3 letters or longer, and an actual dictionary word — anything else doesn't even get scored.
  • It also has to be worth at least 3 points to count at all. Since a wildcard always scores 0, a word like TIE spelled with a wildcard standing in for the E (1+1+0 = 2) is genuine English, but it's still too small to bank — you'll see it flagged red with its true (too-low) point total, not a fake zero.
Working the board

Moving between racks

Next Rack → advances you to the next of the three scrambles, and wraps back around to the first after the third — so nothing is ever off-limits once you've moved on. Revisit an earlier rack any time before the clock runs out.

Modes

Single player vs. multiplayer

Solo
Gerbil Single Player

Ten minutes, three racks generated just for you. Your total score is simply whatever's currently sitting in each rack's guess row, added together — there's no separate "submit." That also means clearing a rack (or moving on with it empty) drops that rack's points back to zero, so leave your best word in place before you move on.

Up to 6 players
Gerbil Multiplayer
  1. Log in and you're placed into a lobby automatically.
  2. Once a second player joins, a 5-minute countdown starts for the whole lobby. Anyone can back out during the wait.
  3. When it hits zero, everyone gets the exact same three racks — same letters, same bonus slots, same wildcards — and a shared 10-minute round begins together.
  4. Unlike single player, only your best-scoring valid word per rack is kept. Trying something bigger and missing never costs you a word you'd already locked in.

A live leaderboard tracks everyone's running total, and each rack shows the best score anyone has found on it so far — just the number to beat, no names attached to it.

Playing well

A few things worth knowing

”

Watch the word display as you build — a word that looks fine can still flip red once you place the last letter, if it turns out not to be in the dictionary.

”

A common letter sitting on a blue (triple) slot can out-score a rare letter sitting on a plain one. Check what's colored before you commit to a word.

”

In multiplayer, swing for something big — only real, valid words ever get kept, so a failed long-shot costs you nothing.

”

In single player, remember your score reflects what's on the board right now. If you clear a rack to chase a better word, its points are gone until you land the next one.

That's the whole game. Three racks, one clock, and whatever words you can find.
